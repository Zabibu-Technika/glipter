About

Built for the places the cloud forgot.

Glipter is an edge-AI sign language engine from Zabibu Technika, led by Tehillah Kachila. It reads sign through any camera, runs on the device, and prioritises Kenyan Sign Language.

Mission

Why this exists

There are not enough human sign language interpreters. In some schools the ratio has been reported as high as one interpreter for three hundred deaf students. Cloud-heavy avatar products cannot reach the rural schools, public kiosks, and low-end phones where the gap is widest.

Glipter takes the opposite path: sign → text, on-device, offline-capable, and built language by language with the communities who use it.

“Does this help a deaf student in Nairobi communicate with their teacher today, better than not having it?”

The question we keep asking.

Pipeline

How it works

  1. 01
    Camera

    getUserMedia, entirely local.

  2. 02
    Landmarks

    MediaPipe Hands, vendored locally, 21 points per hand.

  3. 03
    Features

    Wrist-centred, scale-normalised coordinates + pairwise distances + joint angles.

  4. 04
    Model

    A tiny neural network runs in plain JavaScript. No server, no GPU.

  5. 05
    Grammar

    Letters become words; KSL gets a conservative S-O-V verb move.

Limits

Read this

  • Static handshapes only for ASL/KSL fingerspelling. J and Z need motion.
  • The KSL word model knows four glosses: FATHER, HELLO, IS, MY.
  • BSL is rule-based and experimental. No permissively licensed BSL landmark corpus is bundled yet.
  • No facial grammar, mouthing, or non-manual signals — all of which are part of real sign languages.
  • Not validated by Deaf signers in any language. Do not use in medical, legal, or high-stakes settings.
  • Browser ASR may use an online service; TTS may use a remote voice if no local voice is installed. Glipter does not upload audio itself.
  • Camera access requires HTTPS or localhost. The UI, learn mode, demo, and self-test work without a camera.
Offline

Private by architecture

Glipter ships its own MediaPipe assets, models, and UI. The service worker precaches the app shell on first visit, so later launches work with no network. There is no backend in the recognition path.
